\item \textbf{Response 1:} We thank the reviewer for raising this point, which we
were able to address quantitatively on a revised version of the platform.

\textbf{The concern is well founded, and we can now state the mechanism precisely.}
Under non-connectable undirected advertising, a broadcaster emits at its advertising
interval $T_\mathrm{adv}$ irrespective of how often the application updates the
payload. A receiver therefore observes at most $1/T_\mathrm{adv}$ distinct values per
second from that neighbour: if the publication period $T_\mathrm{pub}$ is shorter
than $T_\mathrm{adv}$, values are overwritten before they are ever transmitted. The
attainable delivery ratio is bounded by
\[
  \rho_{\max} \;=\; \min\!\left(1,\; T_\mathrm{pub}/T_\mathrm{adv}\right),
\]
so a mismatch in $[T_\mathrm{adv}^\mathrm{min}, T_\mathrm{adv}^\mathrm{max}]$ between
agent classes is, directly, a mismatch in the delivery ceiling available to them.

We verified this on the revised platform with a four-point sweep in $T_\mathrm{pub}$
($10$ repeats per point, $120$\,s per run, identical initial conditions across all
$40$ runs, $T_\mathrm{adv}=100$\,ms fixed). Measured delivery tracks $\rho_{\max}$:
the two points at which the bound is active normalise to $0.818$ and $0.826$ of it,
while the unconstrained points reach $0.997$ and $0.962$. The apparent tenfold
collapse in delivery across the sweep is therefore attributable to the advertising
interval and not to the medium; over the same sweep the Wi-Fi transport, which has no
such ceiling, is flat ($0.987 \rightarrow 0.978$).

\textbf{Effect on the original experiments.} Applying this bound to the original
configuration: the publication period was $500$\,ms; the BLE agents advertised at the
nRF52 default of $100$--$150$\,ms, giving $\rho_{\max}=1$; the bridge agents
advertised through the host controller without an explicit interval and therefore
inherited the controller default of $1280$\,ms, giving $\rho_{\max}\approx0.39$.
The ceiling thus differed by agent class, along the same axis the experiment
compares. We consider this a genuine limitation of the original measurements rather
than a second-order effect, and we have repeated the affected experiments on the
revised platform.

\textbf{Remediation.} In the revised platform the advertising interval and the
scan interval/window are configured from a single specification per experiment,
applied to the Raspberry Pi controller through a raw HCI user channel and to the
nRF52 through a configuration frame over the serial link. Both the requested value
and the programmed value (in $0.625$\,ms units) are recorded in the run metadata, so
the parameter is now reported rather than inferred. The BLE and bridge agents in the
revised experiments therefore share identical advertising and scanning parameters.

\textbf{Residual asymmetries.} We note three that remain, for completeness:
(i) transmit power differs between the two radios and cannot be equalised: the
Raspberry Pi's controller reports $+12$\,dBm for advertising but its
advertisements arrive $20$\,dB weaker than the nRF52's at a common receiver of
identical geometry, indicating that the reported figure is nominal rather than
radiated, and the Bluetooth specification provides no means of setting transmit
power for legacy advertising. We note that this asymmetry acts in the opposite
direction to the delivery asymmetry we report, and therefore cannot account for
it; (ii) duplicate filtering is enabled in the nRF52 scanner and disabled in the
Raspberry Pi scanner. We verified experimentally that this asymmetry cannot be
removed by matching the setting: enabling it on the Raspberry Pi reduces delivery
on BLE-only links from $0.768$ to $0.331$ ($10$ repeats each, $t=21.3$), whereas
the nRF52 delivers $0.949$ with the same feature enabled. The two controllers
implement materially different behaviour under the same name, and the
configuration in use is the one that is correct for each; and (iii) the CYW43455 on the Raspberry Pi
shares a single front-end between Wi-Fi and BLE with a packet traffic arbitration
mechanism, which the nRF52, a single-protocol radio, does not have. We find the
effect of (iii) to be measurable and directional, and we report it separately.
