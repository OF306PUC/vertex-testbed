% Reviewer response: the G2 degradation attributed to "heavier communication
% traffic and coexistence effects".
%
% Written after auditing the 174 collected run directories. The short version:
% the traffic half of the attribution does not survive the move from HTTP pull to
% broadcast, and the platform can support a different, stronger explanation that
% needs no new measurement. The coexistence half is supportable but weak.
%
% Numbers below are from runs/{n650,n950,n18-50hz,n18-50hz-scan100}-* and from
% the manifests in experiments/. Nothing here requires a run that does not exist,
% except where explicitly marked as a proposed addition.

\item The degraded performance under $\mathcal{G}_2$ is attributed to heavier
communication traffic and coexistence effects. Please provide quantitative
evidence for this attribution.

\textbf{Response:} The audit prompted by this question changed our explanation.
The traffic argument was inherited from the previous version of the platform and
does not hold on the current one; we replace it with a mechanism we can compute
and, in part, measure.

\textbf{1. Transmitted airtime does not depend on the degree.} On the revised
platform both media are broadcast, so an agent transmits its state once per
publication period irrespective of how many neighbours read it: airtime per node
is $O(1)$ in the degree rather than $O(|\mathcal{N}_i|)$. This is measured
directly, as the per-node transmit count over identical 120~s runs:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
run & agents & advertisers & transmissions per node \\
\hline
\texttt{n650}   &  6 &  4 & 1200 \\
\texttt{n950}   &  9 &  6 & 1201 \\
\texttt{n18-50hz} & 18 & 12 & 1201 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The count equals $T_\mathrm{run}/T_\mathrm{pub}$ in every case. The previous
version of the platform retrieved each neighbour's state with a separate HTTP
request per neighbour per tick, so its traffic genuinely scaled with the degree
and the original attribution was sound for that implementation. Under broadcast it
is not.

\textbf{2. Received airtime does not depend on the degree either.} A BLE scanner
is promiscuous: it occupies the antenna for
$W_\mathrm{scan}/T_\mathrm{scan}$ of the time and reports whatever arrives, so its
receive occupancy is a configuration constant and is the same for a degree-2 and a
degree-4 node in the same manifest. The degree changes which fraction of what was
already received is useful, not how long the receiver listens. The controller
counters show the receiver is saturated by ambient traffic well before ours
arrives:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\hline
run & advertisers & total reports & foreign & decoded as ours \\
\hline
\texttt{n650}   &  4 & 15561 & 13471 & 2090 \\
\texttt{n950}   &  6 & 18376 & 14797 & 3579 \\
\texttt{n18-50hz-scan100} & 12 & 18087 & 12743 & 5344 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Tripling the number of participating advertisers leaves the total report count
essentially unchanged, because the majority of received advertisements are from
unrelated devices in the same band throughout. Consequently the coexistence
exposure discussed above is also independent of the degree: on the Raspberry
Pi it is $W_\mathrm{scan}/T_\mathrm{scan}$ plus the $\approx\!1\%$ advertising
duty, and neither term contains $|\mathcal{N}_i|$.

\textbf{3. What the degree does change.} Three quantities, of which the second is
the one we now advance as the explanation.

\emph{(a) Compounded staleness.} The coordination input is evaluated from the last
value received from each neighbour. If a link delivers a fraction $\rho$ of
published values, and neighbours are treated as independent, the probability that
at least one of $d$ neighbours failed to update within a given publication
interval is $1-\rho^{\,d}$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
measured $\rho$ & source & $d=2$ & $d=4$ \\
\hline
0.79 & nRF receivers, 100\% scan duty    & 0.376 & 0.610 \\
0.60 & nRF$\rightarrow$Pi, saturated     & 0.640 & 0.870 \\
0.40 & nRF receivers, 56.25\% scan duty  & 0.840 & 0.974 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The same links therefore yield a coupling term built on stale data far more often
at degree 4 than at degree 2, with no change in traffic. The independence
assumption is optimistic: correlated loss would make the degree-4 column worse,
not better.

\emph{(b) The stability margin tightens with the degree.} The multi-rate
condition of the Modeling subsection is $5a_{ij} < 2/\lambda_{\max}(L)$, and
$\lambda_{\max}$ is a property of the graph. With the gain held at
$a_{ij}=0.01$ across all three topologies, as it was:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\hline
topology & degree & $\lambda_2$ & $\lambda_{\max}$ & $2/\lambda_{\max}$ & $5a_{ij}$ & margin \\
\hline
$\mathcal{G}_1$ directed ring & 2 & 0.0437 & 4.000 & 0.500 & 0.100 & $5.0\times$ \\
$\mathcal{G}_2$ ring, degree 4 & 4 & 0.2166 & 6.236 & 0.321 & 0.100 & $3.2\times$ \\
$\mathcal{G}_3$ clustered & 2--4 & 0.1392 & 5.618 & 0.356 & 0.100 & $3.6\times$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

$\mathcal{G}_2$ raises $\lambda_{\max}$ by 56\% relative to $\mathcal{G}_1$, so
the admissible gain falls by the same factor while the gain in use does not. The
denser topology is therefore run at $1.56\times$ the effective aggressiveness of
the sparser one, purely as a consequence of its spectrum. This predicts the
reported signature specifically: finite-time convergence still attained, but a
larger error while disagreements and hence the coupling term are large, which is
early in the run. It requires no radio measurement and is computable from the
manifests.

\emph{(c) Host-side work} scales with the degree (report parsing, neighbour-table
update, the coupling sum), but this is processor time rather than antenna time and
is far from any observed limit at these rates.

\textbf{4. What the present data cannot show.} All 174 collected runs use
degree-2 topologies, so we have no hardware comparison of $\mathcal{G}_1$ against
$\mathcal{G}_2$ on the revised platform; the reported $\mathcal{G}_2$ result
belongs to the previous version. Items 1 and 2 above are measured, item 3(a) is
an extrapolation from measured $\rho$, and item 3(b) is computed from the graphs.
We therefore state the traffic explanation as \emph{excluded} rather than
replaced by a measured alternative, and we identify the spectral mechanism as the
leading candidate rather than a demonstrated cause.

The decisive experiment is inexpensive and we propose to add it: a degree-4 arm at
18 agents on the same six hosts, same rates, same seed and same radio
configuration, differing from the existing degree-2 manifest in the edge set
alone. It sits exactly at the four-neighbour limit of the microcontroller
implementation, and it tests 3(a) and 3(b) simultaneously, since the two predict
different signatures: compounded staleness degrades the steady-state error
uniformly, whereas a reduced stability margin degrades the early transient.
